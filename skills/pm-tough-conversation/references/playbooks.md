# Playbooks by conversation type

These are short patterns to shape the prep. They don't replace it.

## Missed deadline / slipping commitment
- **Tell them early.** Bad news gets worse with time. Tell them as soon as you
  know, not on the due date.
- Structure: what we committed → where we are (facts) → why (one line, no
  excuses) → the new realistic date *and the confidence in it* → what we're
  changing so it doesn't happen again → options (a smaller scope by the
  original date, or the full scope later).
- Give one new date you're confident in. Don't give a series of optimistic
  ones.

## Incident / failure (bad data, broken pipeline, wrong numbers delivered)
- Structure: what happened → impact (who saw wrong data, which decisions it
  may have affected) → current status (fixed / mitigated / ongoing) → root
  cause (blameless, systemic) → prevention (specific actions, with dates) →
  what we need from them (for example, re-sending corrected reports to their
  stakeholders).
- With data incidents, the client's biggest fear is often *"did I present
  wrong numbers to my boss?"* Address it directly. Offer a corrected figure
  and a short note they can forward.

## Angry client
- Let them vent first. Don't interrupt and don't defend.
- Acknowledge the emotion and the impact ("I understand this put you in a
  difficult spot with your team") before you get to facts.
- Find the real issue. Anger about a delay is often anger about being
  surprised, or about looking bad.
- Move to the future: "Here's what I propose…", then agree on next steps and
  confirm them in writing.

## Scope pushback / saying no
- Say no to the *timing or approach*, not to the person or the goal: "Yes, we
  can do X. To do it by Friday, we'd move Y to next sprint. Which matters
  more?"
- Use the spec's OUT OF SCOPE list and the decision log as shared ground
  truth, not as a weapon.
- Ladder of Laziness framing: offer the cheaper rung that meets the
  underlying need.

## Budget / price / hours
- Base it on facts: hours used vs planned, and what changed in scope (from the
  change logs).
- Offer options: reduce scope, phase it, or increase the budget. Let them
  choose.
- If commercial terms are involved and the user isn't the account owner,
  bring in whoever owns the contract.

## Feedback to a teammate (or a vendor)
- SBI: **S**ituation → **B**ehavior (observable) → **I**mpact. Then ask for
  their view, then agree on what changes.
- Do it privately and soon after the event. Give one topic per conversation.

## Frameworks behind these
- *Crucial Conversations*: separate facts from stories, make the conversation
  feel safe, and use STATE (Share facts, Tell your story, Ask for theirs, Talk
  tentatively, Encourage testing).
- *Difficult Conversations*: every hard conversation is really three: what
  happened, feelings, and identity.
- *Never Split the Difference*: label emotions ("It seems like…"), use
  calibrated "how/what" questions, and accusation audits.
- SBI (Center for Creative Leadership) for feedback.
